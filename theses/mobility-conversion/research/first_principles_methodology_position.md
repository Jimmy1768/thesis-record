# First Principles Methodology Position

Status: formation-stage methodology position. This is a defended working
position, not accepted empirical evidence or final methodology.

## Core Claim

Mobility Conversion is not a search for the cities with the best transit,
highest GDP, strongest reputation, or most attractive livability profile.

The thesis asks how well a Metropolitan Transport Region converts its
resources, constraints, inherited shape, governance arrangements, and growth
stage into usable access.

The methodological object is therefore a conversion relationship:

```text
capacity / pressure / shape / governance / growth -> usable access
```

This requires three protections:

- the unit must match the real geography of access;
- the outcome must measure usable access, not inputs or reputation;
- the method must expose uncertainty, missingness, and failure instead of
  smoothing them away.

## Boundary Position

The MTR boundary should optimize first for behavioral validity.

Transport access is experienced through actual travel regions. Municipal
boundaries are often too narrow, national boundaries are too broad, and
transport-agency boundaries may reflect institutional authority more than the
geography of daily access.

The current working hierarchy is:

```text
functional travel boundary
official statistical metropolitan boundary
transport governance or service boundary
built-up urban boundary
thesis-specific composite boundary
```

Functional travel boundaries are preferred in principle because they are closest
to the lived geography of access. Official statistical metropolitan boundaries
are the practical fallback when functional travel boundaries are unavailable,
stale, or non-comparable.

Governance and service boundaries should usually be explanatory context, not
the default unit. Built-up boundaries should usually support urban-form
analysis, not define the MTR by themselves.

Composite boundaries may be necessary, but they carry the highest discretion
risk and require written adjustment rules before use.

## Outcome Position

Usable access should begin as a multi-dimensional outcome framework, not a
single composite score.

A single score would force premature weighting and hide real tradeoffs. A
region can have strong job access but weak safety, strong rail coverage but
poor affordability, fast car access but poor universal access, or good average
travel times with severe peripheral exclusion.

Those differences are not noise. They are central to the thesis.

## Tiered Outcome Strategy

The first version should use a two-tier outcome structure.

Tier 1 should contain the most comparable usable-access dimensions:

- access to opportunities;
- network coverage and service availability;
- travel time and reliability;
- transportation safety;
- affordability.

Tier 2 should preserve access-completeness dimensions that are harder to
compare but too important to ignore:

- walking, cycling, and universal access;
- asset condition and resilience;
- service integration and governance-adjacent access barriers;
- informal or weakly documented transport where relevant.

This structure avoids two bad options:

- pretending every important access dimension is globally comparable now;
- narrowing the thesis only to what data-rich formal systems publish cleanly.

## Legibility Risk

The largest current methodological risk is rewarding legibility instead of
mobility conversion.

Some MTRs will have clean feeds, published dashboards, formal accessibility
tools, and detailed agency reports. Others may have important transport systems
that are less formally documented, more fragmented, informal, or harder to map.

If the method only measures what is cleanly published, it may rank
administrative visibility rather than usable access.

Every proposed metric should therefore face this question:

```text
Does this measure real access, or only documented access?
```

Missing data should be classified rather than silently converted into good or
bad performance.

Candidate missingness classes:

- source not found;
- source exists but is not inspectable;
- source exists but is not comparable;
- source boundary does not match the MTR;
- source measures supply rather than access;
- source is too stale;
- source is methodologically unsuitable;
- source is proprietary or opaque.

## Honest Formation Position

The current defended position is:

- use behavioral validity first for MTR boundaries;
- use official statistical metropolitan boundaries as fallback;
- keep governance and built-up boundaries as explanatory/contextual layers;
- begin with a multi-dimensional usable-access framework;
- separate Tier 1 comparable dimensions from Tier 2 access-completeness
  dimensions;
- treat missingness as a classified result;
- do not name candidate MTRs until boundary and outcome gates are accepted;
- do not calculate final scores during candidate formation.

## What This Position Does Not Pretend

This position does not pretend that:

- a perfect global MTR boundary source already exists;
- all important access dimensions are equally measurable;
- data-rich regions are necessarily higher-performing;
- informal or poorly documented systems are unimportant;
- comparability and validity can both be maximized at once;
- a composite index is currently justified;
- missing data is neutral.

The honest v0 task is to define what can be compared cleanly, what cannot, and
why those limits matter for the thesis.

## Decision Dependency

This position should be revisited after the MTR boundary source scan returns
from Deep Research and the underlying sources are independently inspected.
