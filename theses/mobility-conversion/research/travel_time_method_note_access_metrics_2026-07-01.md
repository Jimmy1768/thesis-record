## Core Distinction

An access metric must state which travel-time class it uses.

Allowed formation-stage classes:

- `scheduled_travel_time`
- `observed_travel_time`
- `modelled_travel_time`
- `reliability_adjusted_travel_time`
- `survey_reported_travel_time`
- `mixed_travel_time_with_documented_components`

Do not mix these classes without documenting the component sources and
interpretation.

## Class Definitions

### Scheduled Travel Time

Definition:

- travel time inferred from published schedules, service calendars, stop times,
  frequencies, routes, transfers, and related planned-service data.

Likely source families:

- GTFS Schedule;
- agency timetable data.

Usefulness:

- useful for planned public transport access;
- suitable for first-pass scheduled access analysis where feed quality is
  documented.

Limitations:

- planned service is not delivered service;
- no direct reliability evidence;
- feed completeness, freshness, and mode coverage must be reviewed;
- walking access, transfers, wait-time assumptions, and service-day assumptions
  must be explicit.

### Observed Travel Time

Definition:

- travel time measured from actual operations, traces, probe data, AVL/APC
  systems, archived real-time feeds, surveys, or travel records.

Likely source families:

- archived GTFS Realtime;
- agency operations archives;
- travel surveys;
- road speed/probe data where method is inspectable.

Usefulness:

- closer to experienced travel time than schedules.

Limitations:

- often proprietary, incomplete, or unevenly archived;
- observation window matters;
- source-rich regions may be overrepresented;
- privacy, licensing, and method transparency must be checked.

### Modelled Travel Time

Definition:

- travel time generated by a routing or accessibility model using network,
  schedule, speed, transfer, walking, cycling, road, or impedance assumptions.

Likely source families:

- GTFS Schedule plus routing engine;
- street networks;
- OpenStreetMap or official pedestrian/cycling networks;
- road speed assumptions;
- accessibility model outputs.

Usefulness:

- necessary when observed data are unavailable;
- can combine modes and opportunity inventories.

Limitations:

- model assumptions can dominate the result;
- travel-time thresholds are normative choices;
- modelled paths may ignore safety, comfort, disability access, fare barriers,
  crowding, or real disruptions.

### Reliability-Adjusted Travel Time

Definition:

- scheduled, observed, or modelled travel time adjusted for delay, cancellation,
  disruption, headway variability, or travel-time uncertainty.

Likely source families:

- archived GTFS Realtime;
- agency service-alert archives;
- operations datasets;
- repeated observed travel-time samples.

Usefulness:

- better represents whether access is dependable.

Limitations:

- requires archival evidence;
- live feeds alone are insufficient;
- reliability can be route-, time-, mode-, and operator-specific;
- missing real-time data should not be treated as reliable service.

### Survey-Reported Travel Time

Definition:

- travel time reported by travelers in census, household travel survey,
  commuting survey, or travel diary sources.

Likely source families:

- census commute-time data;
- household travel surveys;
- travel diaries.

Usefulness:

- reflects experienced travel under real conditions.

Limitations:

- often commute-focused;
- may not include non-work trips;
- subject to recall, sampling, and post-2020 commuting caveats;
- may not align with MTR boundaries.

## Minimum Method Fields

Any access metric using travel time must document:

- travel-time class;
- mode scope;
- trip-purpose scope;
- source authority;
- source vintage;
- observation window, if any;
- schedule service day or time period;
- peak/off-peak treatment;
- wait-time assumption;
- transfer penalty or transfer rule;
- walking/egress speed or network assumption;
- accessibility or disability constraint treatment;
- fare or cost inclusion, if any;
- safety constraint inclusion, if any;
- reliability adjustment, if any;
- MTR boundary crosswalk;
- missing service and missing feed handling.

## Pre-Admission Rule

An access metric cannot pass pre-admission if:

- scheduled time is described as observed time;
- live real-time data is treated as archived reliability evidence;
- modelled travel time hides its network, transfer, wait-time, or walking
  assumptions;
- commute-only reported time is treated as all-purpose access;
- proprietary or opaque travel-time data cannot be inspected;
- missing travel-time data is treated as high performance.

## Implication For Job-Access Retest

The first job-access retest should use `scheduled_travel_time` only unless a
documented archive supports reliability adjustment.

Required label if retested:

- `scheduled_public_transport_job_access`

Required exclusions:

- not observed travel time;
- not reliability-adjusted unless archive exists;
- not car access;
- not full access to opportunities;
- not a final usable-access outcome.
