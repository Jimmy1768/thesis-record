# Source Note: UN SDG 11.2.1 Public Transport Access

Date inspected: 2026-06-28

Verification status: directly inspected official United Nations SDG metadata.

Sources:

- United Nations Statistics Division, SDG Indicator 11.2.1 metadata
- URL: `https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-11-02-01.pdf`

## Source Authority

United Nations SDG indicator metadata, with UN-Habitat as the custodian agency
for indicator 11.2.1.

## What The Source Defines

SDG indicator 11.2.1 measures the proportion of the population with convenient
access to public transport.

The indicator is relevant to Mobility Conversion because it explicitly connects:

- population denominators;
- public transport stop or station access;
- walking-distance thresholds;
- vulnerable-population reporting;
- urban access monitoring;
- limitations of public transport availability data.

## Outcome Dimension Fit

Primary fit:

- network coverage and service availability.

Secondary fit:

- access to opportunities, if linked to destination and travel-time analysis;
- walking, cycling, and universal access, if pedestrian network and user-group
  constraints are explicitly included;
- affordability, only if paired with fare and income data.

## Usefulness For Mobility Conversion

This source is a strong precedent for measuring public transport access as an
access outcome input rather than as a reputation claim.

It is useful for:

- defining public transport access catchment concepts;
- separating population-with-access from total MTR population;
- documenting disadvantaged groups and accessibility concerns;
- forcing explicit treatment of the walking connection between people and
  service.

## Limitations

- The indicator is not an MTR boundary source.
- Stop or station proximity is not enough to establish usable access.
- Frequency, span, reliability, crowding, fare burden, safety, accessibility,
  and destination reach may require additional sources.
- Informal transport and non-scheduled services may be difficult to represent.
- Global reporting may simplify local network conditions.
- Reported urban geographies may not align with Thesis 2 MTR boundaries.

## Suitability Judgment

Suitability: `indicator_source_family`.

Candidate outcome dimensions:

- `network_coverage_and_service_availability`;
- `walking_cycling_and_universal_access`, as a partial input only;
- `access_to_opportunities`, only when combined with destination and
  travel-time sources.

Not suitable as:

- MTR denominator;
- complete usable-access outcome;
- final metric without boundary, service-quality, and population-crosswalk
  review.

## Methodology Implication

SDG 11.2.1 should be treated as a source-family precedent for public transport
access coverage. It should not become the full Thesis 2 outcome because usable
access requires quality, time, affordability, safety, reliability, and
opportunity-reach tests beyond stop proximity.
