# Source Note: WHO Global Road Safety 2023 As Safety Input

Date inspected: 2026-06-30

Verification status: directly inspected official WHO page for the Global status
report on road safety 2023.

Sources:

- World Health Organization, "Global status report on road safety 2023"
- URL: `https://www.who.int/teams/social-determinants-of-health/safety-and-mobility/global-status-report-on-road-safety-2023`

## Source Authority

World Health Organization road safety reporting.

## What The Source Defines

The WHO global road safety report family provides international road-safety
context, including country-level road traffic death estimates and policy,
vehicle, infrastructure, and post-crash care context.

For Mobility Conversion, the relevant source-family function is global
safety-context benchmarking, not direct MTR measurement.

## Outcome Dimension Fit

Primary fit:

- transportation safety.

Secondary fit:

- walking, cycling, and universal access, where vulnerable road-user context is
  available;
- governance fit, as contextual explanation only, where national road-safety
  policy differences affect local outcomes.

## Usefulness For Mobility Conversion

WHO road-safety reporting is useful for:

- establishing that road death and injury risk are access-quality issues;
- identifying national safety context around candidate MTRs;
- comparing broad country-level safety environments;
- warning that local MTR safety cannot be inferred from service coverage or
  network supply.

## Limitations

- WHO reporting is primarily national, not MTR boundary aligned.
- Country-level estimates can hide large local differences.
- Fatality counts do not measure all serious injuries, perceived safety,
  harassment, near misses, or suppressed walking and cycling demand.
- National safety context is not the same as MTR transport quality.
- Exposure denominators such as trips, passenger-km, vehicle-km, or walking and
  cycling volumes must come from other sources.

## Suitability Judgment

Suitability: `context_source_family`.

Candidate outcome dimensions:

- `transportation_safety`, as national context only.

Not suitable as:

- MTR denominator;
- standalone MTR safety metric;
- final comparable safety score;
- substitute for local crash, injury, transit safety, or exposure data.

## Methodology Implication

WHO road-safety data should be used to preserve global safety context and to
avoid pretending that transport access is only about speed, coverage, or
affordability. It should not be used alone to evaluate an MTR because the
geography and exposure basis are too coarse.
